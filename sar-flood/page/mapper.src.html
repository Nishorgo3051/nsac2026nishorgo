Bangladesh SAR Flood Mapper

Finds new open floodwater from radar, either Sentinel-1 (C-band) or NISAR (L-band), and hands it on as GeoJSON polygons. It only maps the water. Deciding where boats, food and medicine go is a separate module that reads this output.

Scientific-integrity caveat

It sees open water, and misses water with something standing in it

These areas are missed, not falsely flagged. That is the opposite of the failure people usually expect. Every area this tool reports is a lower bound, and flooded settlements, where people actually are, are the most likely to be under-counted. Each output polygon carries detects: "open_water_only", so the resource module can't lose this caveat.

Submission write-up

Paste-ready methodology
We map new open floodwater with Sentinel-1 radar, which sees through monsoon cloud, following the UN-SPIDER Recommended Practice for SAR flood mapping in Google Earth Engine. For a chosen area we compare a pre-flood and a during-flood VH radar image taken from the same orbit track, smooth radar speckle with a 50 m median filter, and flag pixels whose backscatter dropped sharply, using a threshold chosen automatically by Otsu's method (with UN-SPIDER's default of 1.25 as a fallback). We then remove permanent rivers and ponds using the JRC Global Surface Water dataset, remove steep slopes where radar shadow can mimic water, clean away isolated pixels, and export each flooded patch as a polygon with its area and image date. The method detects only open water: flooded crops, trees and built-up streets often turn brighter to radar rather than darker (the "double bounce" effect), so they are missed, which makes our flooded area a lower bound that most likely under-counts flooding in villages and towns. The same pipeline also runs on NISAR L-band radar (GCOV product, HH channel) streamed from NASA's Alaska Satellite Facility, reading only the area of interest from each multi-gigabyte file; this NISAR path is experimental until it has been checked against Sentinel-1 on the same flood.

A validation sentence gets added here after the Feni run, written from the real result. It will not be written before that result exists.

How it works

Radar measures how much of its own microwave pulse bounces back, in decibels. In the VH channel, calm water is the darkest thing in the scene. A flood shows up as ground that got darker between two passes.

  2. 2One image per period9 Aug vs 21 Aug 2024Frames from the same pass are stitched into one picture for the baseline window and one for the flood window.
  3. 3Smooth the specklemedian · 50 mRaw radar is grainy because tiny echoes add up or cancel at random. A median smooths the grain but keeps riverbanks sharp.
  4. 4Measure the changeduring ÷ beforeBoth values are negative dB, so the ratio goes above 1 when the ground got darker: −22 ÷ −15 = 1.47 for a field that flooded.
  5. 5Remove permanent water and slopesJRC > 50 % · slope < 5°Rivers and ponds are already dark and flicker with the wind. Hill shadows look like water, and Feni borders the Tripura hills.
  6. 6ThresholdOtsu, else 1.25Pixels above the cut become flood. Only open water shows here; see the caveat above. If Otsu lands outside 1.1–2.0, UN-SPIDER's 1.25 is used and the output says so.
  7. 7Clean upopening · ≥ 8 pxShrink every patch by a pixel and grow it back, so lone pixels vanish. Then drop patches smaller than 8 pixels.
  8. 8Turn into polygonsGeoJSON · km² in UTMOne polygon per separate patch, measured in metres, not degrees, because a square degree isn't a fixed size on the ground.
  9. 9Draw the picturePNGBefore, during and the flood mask side by side, so it can be demoed without GIS software.

Output contract

The promise the resource-coordination module can rely on. The file out/<area>_flood_<date>.geojson is a FeatureCollection in longitude/latitude (EPSG:4326), with one Feature per patch of new open water, largest first. An empty collection means no new open water was found. It isn't an error.

PropertyTypeMeaning
aoi_nametextArea analysed, e.g. "Feni"
sensortext"Sentinel-1 C-band VH" or "NISAR L-band HH". Thresholds differ by sensor.
acquisition_datetextDate of the during-flood image, YYYY-MM-DD in UTC; comma-separated if several
baseline_datetextDate of the pre-flood image, same format
area_km2numberArea of this patch, measured in metres (UTM)

Alongside it: out/<area>_flood_<date>.png, the before / during / flood picture.

Run it

earthengine authenticate              # once; signs in with Google in your browser
set EE_PROJECT=your-cloud-project-id  # a project registered for non-commercial Earth Engine

run.cmd test_flood_extent.py          # local checks, no sign-in needed
run.cmd flood_extent.py               # Sentinel-1: reproduces the Feni 2024 run
run.cmd nisar_flood.py                # NISAR: also needs Earthdata; the Chattogram 2026 test
run.cmd page\build.py                 # rebuilds this page after a code change

The helper runs each script in the project's own Python environment, so the libraries are already installed. When something goes wrong the scripts say so in plain words: Earth Engine refusing an area that is too large, the two date windows being the wrong way round, or a mask grid that does not line up.

Register an Earth Engine project for free at code.earthengine.google.com/register, and a NASA Earthdata account at urs.earthdata.nasa.gov. For NISAR, set EARTHDATA_USERNAME and EARTHDATA_PASSWORD, use a .netrc file, or type them when the script asks. To map another area, change the settings block at the top of either script.

Test case: south Chattogram, July 2026 floods

Area
Satkania, Lohagara, Chandanaish and Banshkhali upazilas, 1,099 km², named among the worst hit.
The event
Rain began 5 Jul 2026, and 412 mm fell on 7 Jul, Chattogram's heaviest in 43 years. By 12 Jul water covered 59 upazilas in 7 districts, with about 1.28 million people affected.
Radar passes
NISAR track 69, ascending, same 40 MHz dual-pol mode: baseline 30 Jun 2026, flood 12 Jul 2026 (05:21 local, the next morning). Frames 12 and 13 together cover the area. The live search finds exactly these four files, about 20 GB in total, of which only the area is read.

No published flooded-area figure for this event has turned up yet, so plausibility rests on four checks:

  • Clear new water in all four upazilas on 12 Jul.
  • A cross-sensor check: run the Sentinel-1 script on the same area and dates (Sentinel-1A and 1C both fly in 2026) and compare.
  • No patches on the hill slopes east of Satkania.
  • At least 95% data coverage on both dates. The script warns if it's lower.
